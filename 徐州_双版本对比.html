01 到站先别急着找景点Don't rush for the sights

徐州东站的站台很长，你从车上下来，风里带一点煤灰味，也带一点面香。

这座城不靠好看取胜，它的本事是"通"——京沪和陇海两条铁路在这儿交叉，南来北往的人都得经过。

你先坐一次公交吧，票价便宜，路线绕，正好把老城和新区串起来看一遍。

别急着奔景点。

徐州的好东西大多埋在地下，或者藏在一碗汤里。

The platform at Xuzhou East is long, and when you step off the train the air carries a trace of coal dust and a trace of bread.

This city does not win on looks; its talent is being a junction, where the Beijing–Shanghai and Longhai lines cross and anyone heading north or south passes through.

Take a bus first — fares are cheap, the route wanders, and you get the old town and the new district in one loop.

Don't rush for the sights.

Most of what Xuzhou keeps is underground, or in a bowl of soup.

02 山肚子里是空的The hill is hollow

龟山不高，你爬上去只用几分钟。

可山肚子里是空的，这是一座西汉楚王的墓，直接从山体里凿出来。

两条甬道又长又直，墙壁打磨得很平。

你走在墓道里，头顶是整块石头，两千多年前的工匠一锤一凿把这座山掏成了房子。

出来以后天光刺眼，你会愣一下，才想起刚才在地下待了多久。

Turtle Hill is not high; the climb takes you only a few minutes.

But the hill is hollow — this is the tomb of a Western Han king of Chu, cut straight out of the rock.

Two passageways run long and true, their walls smoothed flat.

You walk the corridor with solid stone overhead; craftsmen more than two thousand years ago hammered this hill into a house.

Daylight is sharp when you come out, and you pause a moment, remembering how long you were underground.

03 四千个小兵Four thousand small soldiers

狮子山楚王陵外有一片俑坑，兵马俑排成方阵，一个个只有几十厘米高。

它们没有西安那种气势。

可你蹲下来看，每张脸都不一样。

这是西汉楚国自己的军队，缩成陶土，替一位王守了两千年。

展馆里人不多，你可以慢慢看那些发式、铠甲，还有马。

Outside the Chu royal tomb at Lion Hill lies a pit of figurines, drawn up in formation, each only a few dozen centimetres tall.

They lack the grandeur of Xi'an.

But when you crouch down, every face is different.

This was the army of the Han state of Chu, shrunk into clay, keeping watch for a king for two thousand years.

The hall is rarely crowded, so you can take your time over the hairstyles, the armour, the horses.

04 云龙湖：一湖水压住了城Yunlong Lake holds the city down

云龙湖很大，你沿湖走，一边是水，一边是云龙山。

本地人在湖边钓鱼、遛娃、跳舞，你加入哪一样都不突兀。

山上有放鹤亭。

苏轼在这儿做过官，写过文章，说鹤飞走了就不回来了。

山不深，林子密，你走到半山腰回头，能看见整片水面。

Yunlong Lake is big; walk along it and there is water on one side, Cloud Dragon Hill on the other.

Locals fish, walk children and dance by the water, and nothing looks odd if you join in.

On the hill stands a pavilion for releasing cranes.

Su Shi served here and wrote about it; the cranes, he said, flew off and never came back.

The hill is not deep and the trees are thick — halfway up, turn around and the whole lake is below you.

05 户部山的老房子Old houses on Hubu Hill

户部山是一片老民居，青砖、小院、窄巷子，抬头是翘起来的屋脊。

崔家大院值得进去，院子一套套往里套，像迷宫。

你走累了就坐在门槛上，有老人晒太阳，有猫从墙头过去。

回龙窝在旁边，修得新一些。

入夜灯亮起来好看。

Hubu Hill is a quarter of old houses: grey brick, small courtyards, narrow lanes, and roof ridges that curl upward.

The Cui family compound is worth going in, courtyard opening into courtyard like a maze.

You can sit on a doorstep when your legs give out; old men take the sun and a cat crosses the wall.

Huilongwo is next door, restored more recently.

It is at its best once the lamps come on.

06 戏马台：项羽在这儿遛过马Where Xiang Yu drilled his horses

项羽定都彭城，在城南的山岗上筑台看兵马操练。

那地方现在叫戏马台，台不高，你站上去看得见一片屋顶。

楚汉相争的故事，本地人讲得比书熟。

谁赢谁输，他们各有各的说法。

台下有石刻和碑，风吹过，树响，别的就安静了。

Xiang Yu made Pengcheng his capital and raised a terrace on the ridge south of town to watch his horses drill.

The place is still called Ximatai; the terrace is not high, and you stand on it and see a field of rooftops.

Locals tell the Chu–Han contest better than books do.

Who won, who lost — everyone has a version.

Below are stone carvings and steles; the wind moves the trees, and everything else goes quiet.

07 城市最近的一道疤The city's most recent scar

城南有一座纪念塔和一座纪念馆。

名字很重：淮海战役。

1948 年的冬天到 1949 年的年初，这里打了六十多天。

你进馆里看地图和旧照片，人很多，声音会低下去。

出来是一片林子和台阶，孩子在上面跑，老人在下面坐。

South of the centre stand a memorial tower and a museum.

The name is heavy: the Huaihai Campaign.

From the winter of 1948 into the first days of 1949, the fighting here ran more than sixty days.

You go in to look at maps and old photographs; crowds come and voices drop.

Outside are trees and steps, children running above and old men sitting below.

08 黄河走了，留下一道故道The river left, and left a bed behind

黄河曾经从这里走，1855 年它改道北上，留下一道干掉的老河床。

现在故道上种了果树，修了路，你骑车过去，路面很平。

潘安湖更特别。

那儿原本是煤矿塌陷地，地沉下去，积了水，后来做成湿地公园。

一片塌陷区能变回一片有鸟的水面，这件事本身就值得看。

The Yellow River once ran through here; in 1855 it shifted north and left behind a dry old bed.

The old course is planted with fruit trees and laid with paths, and you cycle it on a surface that is perfectly flat.

Pan'an Lake is stranger still.

It was mine subsidence — the ground sank and water gathered — and it has since been made into a wetland park.

That a collapsed pit can become a lake full of birds is worth seeing on its own.

09 地锅鸡和一碗啥汤Pot chicken and a bowl of sha tang

徐州的吃很实在，地锅鸡是一口铁锅，鸡肉贴着锅边，饼子半泡在汤里。

啥汤要趁热喝，胡椒味重，配油条或者烙馍。

入伏以后本地人吃伏羊，一碗羊汤下去，出一身汗。

你不一定都吃得惯。

但量足，价实，不会让你饿着走。

Xuzhou food is solid; pot chicken comes in an iron pan, the meat against the rim, flatbread half-drowned in the sauce.

Sha tang should be drunk hot, heavy on pepper, with fried dough or a griddle cake.

When the dog days arrive, locals eat mutton, and one bowl leaves you sweating.

You may not take to all of it.

But portions are generous, prices fair, and you will not leave hungry.

实用贴士：啥汤（读 shá）多为早点摊，卖完就收摊，起早才吃得到；地锅鸡在城郊的店做得更足，市区也有不少分店。
Tip:Sha tang is sold at breakfast stalls that close once the pot is empty — come early. Pot chicken is heartier at places on the outskirts, though the city has plenty of branches.
10 几件要紧事A few things that matter

徐州的景点分得散，汉墓在西北，湖在西南，纪念塔在南边。

你按片区看，别来回穿城。

火车站有两个，徐州东站跑高铁，老徐州站离老城近，买票前先看清楚是哪一个。

城里共享单车多，短途骑最省事，夏天晒，冬天风硬。

博物馆值得一去，徐州博物馆和汉画像石馆挨着，能看半天。

Xuzhou's sights are spread out — the Han tombs in the northwest, the lake in the southwest, the memorial in the south.

Take them by district; don't keep crossing the city.

There are two railway stations: Xuzhou East takes the high-speed trains, the old Xuzhou station is closer to the old town, so check which one before you buy.

Shared bikes are everywhere and best for short hops; summers are hot, and winters have a hard wind.

The museums are worth it — the Xuzhou Museum and the Han stone-relief gallery sit side by side and can fill half a day.

实用贴士：徐州博物馆与汉画像石艺术馆相邻，凭身份证可领免费票；狮子山楚王陵、龟山汉墓在西北郊，公交可达但班次不密，往返要留富余时间。
Tip:The Xuzhou Museum and the Han Stone Relief art gallery are neighbours — bring ID for a free ticket. The Chu tomb at Lion Hill and the Turtle Hill tomb are in the northwest suburbs: buses reach them, but not often, so allow slack.
11 离开的时候On leaving

徐州不留人，它太像个路口了，人来人往，谁都只是路过。

可你坐上车以后会发现，那些墓、那些兵、那锅鸡，还在脑子里。

这座城把汉朝埋在地下，把战役写在碑上，把日子过在湖边和汤碗里。

下次再来也行。

不来，也说得过去。

Xuzhou does not hold people; it is too much a crossroads, and everyone is only passing through.

But once you are on the train, the tombs, the soldiers, that pan of chicken — they stay with you.

This city buried the Han underground, wrote its battles on stone, and lives its days by the lake and in a bowl of soup.

Come again, fine.

Never come back, also fine.

01 一座以"通"为性格的城A city whose character is connection

京沪与陇海两条干线在徐州交叉，这个地理事实塑造了它两千多年来的性格：这里先是一条路，后来才成为一座城。

从车站出来，风里混着煤灰与面食的气味，两种气味都属于这座长期以煤和麦子为底子的城市。

公元前 206 年，项羽自立为西楚霸王，把都城立在彭城，也就是今天的徐州，这一带第一次成为天下的中心之一。

城的历史一层压着一层，最上面是铁路与高架，往下才是楚王凿在岩层里的墓道。

Two trunk lines, Beijing–Shanghai and Longhai, cross at Xuzhou, and that single geographic fact shaped its character for more than two thousand years: this was a road before it was a city.

Out of the station the air carries coal dust and cooking dough, both of them smells of a city built on coal and wheat.

In 206 BC Xiang Yu declared himself hegemon of Western Chu and set his capital at Pengcheng, modern Xuzhou, making this corner of the plain one of the centres of the realm for the first time.

The history lies in layers: railways and flyovers on top, and beneath them the tomb passages a king of Chu cut into the rock.

02 把一座山凿成一座房子A hill hollowed into a house

龟山的海拔不足百米，从山脚走到墓口只要几分钟，山的内部却被整个掏空，凿出了墓室、甬道与天井。

这是西汉某一代楚王的陵墓，当年的工匠不砌砖、不打夯，而是顺着岩层往里凿，把山体本身当成了建筑材料。

两条甬道各长 56 米，彼此平行，凿得笔直，站在入口就能望见另一端的光。

公元前 2 世纪的工匠靠锤与凿，在没有机械的条件下完成这一切；出墓口时天光很亮，人要几秒钟才能重新适应地面。

Turtle Hill rises less than a hundred metres; the walk from its foot to the tomb mouth takes minutes, yet the interior is entirely hollowed out into chambers, passageways and light wells.

This is the tomb of one of the Western Han kings of Chu. The builders laid no brick and rammed no earth — they followed the strata inward and used the hill itself as their material.

Two parallel passageways run 56 metres each, cut so straight that from the entrance you can see daylight at the far end.

Craftsmen of the second century BC did all of it with hammer and chisel and no machinery. The light at the exit is fierce, and it takes a few seconds to readjust to the surface.

03 坑里的四千余件陶俑Four thousand clay soldiers in a pit

狮子山楚王陵外的陪葬俑坑出土了 4000 余件陶制兵马俑，个头只有几十厘米，排成整齐的方阵。

它们没有秦俑的体量，却因为小而保留了更多细节：不同的发髻、不同的眉眼、不同形制的甲片。

这是西汉楚国自己的军队被缩成陶土，埋在王陵之外，继续为一位王守着他的地下朝廷。

1994 年的发掘让这片俑坑重见天日，如今它在原地建馆，观众可以贴着坑边往下看那些两千多年前的队列。

The pits beside the Chu royal tomb at Lion Hill yielded more than 4,000 clay warriors, each only a few dozen centimetres tall, drawn up in neat formation.

They lack the scale of the Qin figures, but their smallness preserved detail: different topknots, different eyes, different patterns of armour plate.

It is the army of the Han state of Chu shrunk into clay and buried outside the royal tomb, still keeping guard over a king's court beneath the ground.

Excavation in 1994 brought the pits back into daylight, and a hall now stands over them, close enough to look down on ranks drawn up two thousand years ago.

04 湖与山：城的一片肺Lake and hill: the city's lung

云龙湖的水面接近 6 平方公里，风从水面过来时带着明显的凉意，把城里的热气往下压了一截。

湖南岸的云龙山海拔只有 142 米，却因为平地突兀而显得比数字更高，山上的放鹤亭与北宋文学家苏轼有关。

他在此任知州时写下《放鹤亭记》，记的是一位隐者与两只鹤，一个关于放与归的小故事。

湖岸没有围起来的景区感，钓鱼的人、推婴儿车的人、跳舞的人共用同一条岸线，谁也不占谁的地。

Yunlong Lake covers close to 6 square kilometres, and the wind coming off it carries a distinct coolness that pushes the city's heat down a notch.

Cloud Dragon Hill on the southern shore stands only 142 metres high, but it rises so abruptly from flat ground that it feels taller than the figure suggests; the Pavilion for Releasing Cranes up there is tied to the Song writer Su Shi.

Serving as prefect here, he wrote an essay on that pavilion about a hermit and two cranes — a small story about letting go and coming home.

The shore has none of the fenced-off feel of a scenic zone. Anglers, people pushing prams and dancers share the same strip of waterfront without competing for it.

05 户部山：择高而居的人家Hubu Hill: those who built on high ground

户部山一片明清民居依坡而建，青砖小院层层叠叠，屋脊两端翘起，是这一带民居最明显的标记。

崔家大院是其中保存较完整的一组，院落一进套一进，你走进去，很快就会迷失方向。

明清两代，因黄河水患频仍、漕运又带来财货，富户与官宦择高而居，房子越盖越密，坡度也越来越陡。

旁边的回龙窝经过整体修缮，砖是新的，巷子是旧的，入夜灯亮起来时最好看。

The Ming and Qing houses on Hubu Hill climb the slope, courtyard stacked above courtyard, their roof ridges cocked up at both ends — the signature of domestic architecture in this part of the plain.

The Cui compound is the most complete group among them, one courtyard opening into the next; go in and you lose your bearings quickly.

Through the Ming and Qing, with the Yellow River flooding often and the canal carrying goods and money, wealthy families and officials chose high ground, building denser and steeper as space ran out.

Huilongwo next door has been restored wholesale: new brick, old lanes, and at its best once the lamps come on.

06 戏马台：霸王与他的马Ximatai: the hegemon and his horses

项羽在彭城定都之后，于城南的山岗上筑台，观看兵马操练，那座台就是今天的戏马台。

台并不高。站上去能看到的只是一片连绵的屋顶，以及更远处被高架切断的地平线。

楚汉相争在徐州留下密集的地名，九里山相传是古战场，本地人对这段历史的熟悉程度往往超过课本。

台上风大，树声盖过人声，碑刻上的字被几百年来的手摸得发亮。

After making Pengcheng his capital, Xiang Yu raised a terrace on the ridge south of the city to watch his horses drill; that terrace is today's Ximatai, the horse-playing platform.

It is not high. From the top you see only a continuous field of rooftops and, further off, a horizon cut by flyovers.

The Chu–Han contest left a thick scatter of place names around Xuzhou — Jiuli Mountain is said to be an old battlefield — and locals usually know the story better than their textbooks did.

The wind is strong up there, the trees louder than the visitors, and the characters on the steles are polished bright by centuries of hands.

07 城南的塔与六十六天The tower in the south and sixty-six days

1948 年 11 月至 1949 年 1 月，以徐州为中心的这片平原上打了 66 天，双方投入的兵力超过百万。

城南凤凰山东麓的淮海战役烈士纪念塔与纪念馆建于此后，塔身高而素白，从城里多处都能望见。

馆内的沙盘、电报与照片把那 66 天拆开来讲，进展厅的人通常会把声音放低。

塔下的台阶上坐满歇脚的人，孩子在平台上奔跑，这份日常与纪念地的肃穆并不真正冲突。

From November 1948 to January 1949, the fighting on this plain around Xuzhou ran for 66 days, with more than a million troops committed between the two sides.

The Huaihai Campaign memorial tower and museum were later built on the eastern foot of Phoenix Mountain south of the city; the tower is tall and plain white, visible from many parts of town.

Inside, sand tables, telegrams and photographs take those 66 days apart piece by piece, and most people lower their voices once they are in the hall.

The steps below are full of people resting, children running on the platform — an ordinariness that does not really clash with the solemnity of the place.

08 故道与塌陷区An old bed and a sunken field

1855 年黄河在铜瓦厢决口改道向北，留在徐州境内的旧河道从此断流，成为今天所说的黄河故道。

故道河床高于两侧地面，是当年泥沙长年堆积的结果，如今种着成片果树，也修了可以骑行的绿道。

潘安湖是同一片土地上的另一个故事：这里原是煤矿采空塌陷区，地面下沉后积水成塘。

从塌陷地到水鸟聚集的湿地，中间没有奇迹，只有几十年连续的回填、引水与植被恢复。

In 1855 the Yellow River broke out at Tongwaxiang and shifted its course north; the channel left inside Xuzhou ran dry and became what is now called the old Yellow River course.

That bed sits higher than the land on either side, the accumulated silt of centuries, and today it carries orchards and a greenway built for cycling.

Pan'an Lake tells the opposite story on the same ground: this was coal-mining subsidence, where the surface sank and water pooled in the hollows.

From a collapsed pit to a wetland full of waterbirds there was no miracle, only decades of backfilling, channelling water in and letting vegetation return.

09 铁锅、胡椒与伏羊Iron pot, pepper, and dog-day mutton

地锅鸡的做法是把柴鸡与贴饼放进同一口铁锅焖熟，饼的一半浸在汤汁里，另一半贴着锅壁烤出焦壳。

啥汤是本地早点，用鸡汤、麦仁与大量胡椒熬成，浓稠辛香，通常配油条或烙馍一同吃下。

每年入伏之后，徐州人要吃"伏羊"，一碗羊汤下肚，出透一身汗，据说能把湿气逼出来；头一回喝的人，多半会被胡椒呛到。

这些菜的共同点是不精致但分量足，价格也低，一顿饭吃得人不得不放慢脚步。

Pot chicken is made by stewing free-range chicken and flatbread in the same iron pan: half the bread soaks in the sauce, half bakes against the wall of the pot into a crust.

Sha tang is the local breakfast, simmered from chicken stock, wheat berries and a great deal of pepper until thick and hot, and eaten with fried dough or a griddle cake.

Once the dog days begin, Xuzhou eats its "dog-day mutton" — a bowl of mutton soup that leaves you sweating hard, said to drive out damp; on a first bowl the pepper probably makes you cough.

What these dishes share is a lack of refinement and a surplus of quantity, at prices low enough that a meal forces you to slow down.

实用贴士：啥汤（读 shá）多为早点摊，卖完即收，起早才吃得到；地锅鸡在城郊的店做得更足，市区也有不少分店。
Tip:Sha tang is sold at breakfast stalls that close once the pot is empty, so come early. Pot chicken is heartier at places on the outskirts, though the city has plenty of branches.
10 方向与车站Directions and stations

徐州的景点分散在城市的不同方向：汉墓群在西北郊，云龙湖在西南，淮海战役纪念建筑在南郊。

高铁主要停靠徐州东站，老徐州站离老城更近，两站之间有地铁相连，你买票时先确认是哪一个站。

市内共享单车数量充足，短途骑行比打车更省时间，夏季日晒强烈，冬季北风很硬。

徐州博物馆与汉画像石艺术馆相邻，画像石上的车马、庖厨与神话图像几乎是汉代的百科全书，值得留够时间。

Xuzhou's sights point in different directions: the Han tombs in the northwest suburbs, Yunlong Lake in the southwest, the Huaihai memorials in the south.

High-speed trains mostly stop at Xuzhou East; the old Xuzhou station sits closer to the old town, and a metro line links the two — check which one the ticket says.

Shared bikes are plentiful and faster than a taxi over short distances; the sun is fierce in summer and the north wind hard in winter.

The Xuzhou Museum and the Han stone-relief gallery are neighbours, and the chariots, kitchens and mythological figures carved on those slabs amount to an encyclopedia of the Han — allow real time for them.

实用贴士：徐州博物馆与汉画像石艺术馆相邻，凭身份证可领免费票，两处加起来能看半天；狮子山楚王陵与龟山汉墓在西北郊，公交可达但班次不密。
Tip:The Xuzhou Museum and the Han Stone Relief art gallery are neighbours, free with ID, and together can fill half a day. The Chu tomb at Lion Hill and the Turtle Hill tomb are in the northwest suburbs: reachable by bus, but not frequently.
11 一座不急着讨好谁的城A city in no hurry to please

徐州很少被当作目的地本身，更多时候它是车票上的一个换乘点，人们在此下车、转车，随即离开。

但地下埋着的汉墓、湖边吹过的风、铁锅里翻滚的贴饼，会在你离开之后慢慢浮上来。

这座城把最辉煌的部分藏在地下，把最沉重的部分刻在碑上，把最日常的部分留在湖岸与早点摊上。

它不着急。

它既没有急着讨好谁，也不担心被人错过；这种不慌不忙，大概是它在两千多年里反复重建之后留下的脾气。

Xuzhou is seldom a destination in itself; more often it is a transfer point printed on a ticket, where people get off, change trains and leave again.

But the tombs underground, the wind off the lake and the bread turning in an iron pot surface slowly after you have gone.

The city keeps its most brilliant part underground, carves its heaviest part on stone, and leaves its most ordinary part on the lakeshore and at the breakfast stalls.

It is in no hurry.

It neither rushes to please nor fears being passed over — a composure that is probably the temper left behind by two thousand years of rebuilding.
